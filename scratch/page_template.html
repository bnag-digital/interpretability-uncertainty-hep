Explainer disagreement against a measured noise floor

Five taggers, five seeds each, on one shared split of the hls4ml 150-particle dataset. The question is whether two explainers disagreeing about a model means anything, or whether it sits inside the noise you get from simply retraining that model.

The result

Every comparison is drawn against the grey band, which runs from that pair's retraining ceiling up to perfect agreement. A point inside the band is a disagreement too small to distinguish from retraining the same model with a different seed. A point to the left of it is a real disagreement.

Read with care

The EFN rows sit on a 3-feature axis, above the dashed line. With three items a Kendall tau can only take the values ±1 and ±1/3, so an EFN ceiling of exactly 1.000 means five seeds happened to agree on one ordering of three things — not that the explanation is stable. Those rows are a resolution limit, not a finding, and the figure separates them so they cannot be read against the 16-feature rows.

The models underneath

None of the above means anything if the taggers themselves were not trained properly, and getting that right took three rounds of correction.

What the training rounds cost

Three separate times, a spread that looked like seed-to-seed variation turned out to be where training stopped. This is a methodological result in its own right: a floor measured with sloppy stopping criteria is wrong in an unpredictable direction.

The GNN row is the interesting one. The prediction, written down in advance, was that removing truncation would shrink its spread. It grew instead, because one seed in five converges legitimately into a worse optimum. The EFN does the same thing at the same training size.

Limits to state, not hide

  • Five seeds estimates a two-mode distribution badly. Two of the five models have one seed landing low and four clustered tightly, so the spread is set by a single run. More seeds would be needed to say how often that happens.
  • The floor is measured on the aggregate over 500 test jets. Per-row explainer noise is averaged away before the correlation is taken, so this is a floor on the dataset-level ranking, not on any single jet's explanation. A per-jet floor would be larger.
  • Row choice is its own variance level. Resampling which 500 jets get explained moves the ranking less than retraining for nine of ten pairs — but for BDT SHAP the two effects are the same size. Going to 1000 rows changes almost nothing.
